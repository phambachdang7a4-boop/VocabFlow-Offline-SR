# Historical portrait reference review

Reviewed the 50 historical portraits (the 44 entries in
`collection-historical-artworks.ts` plus the six separate historical collection
entries). The Hồ Chí Minh bonus statue is outside this 50-figure set.

References below are museum/collection records, archival or contemporary
portraits, and catalogued historical depictions. Where no secure likeness
survives, the reference can support clothing and portrait conventions, but not
an exact face. Those portraits are treated as idealized busts rather than
documentary reconstructions.

## Outcome

The 50 portraits were reviewed. Cleopatra VII was the only clear, correctable
period-regalia mismatch. Her revised image keeps the marble style and 768×768
format while replacing the Egyptian headdress with Hellenistic hair and a low
diadem. Other portraits were retained because their period cues are consistent,
or because reliable sources do not establish an exact likeness.

Collection details identify uncertain or conventional likenesses as idealized
depictions, rather than documentary portraits. This applies to Archimedes,
Confucius, Pythagoras, Hippocrates, Homer, Hannibal, Euclid, Seneca, Ptolemy,
and Sun Tzu. Ptolemy's 1476 image is a later scholarly convention, not a secure
likeness. Secure contemporary and established portrait sources keep their
existing labels and do not receive an uncertainty warning.
